Before you click anything

The opening line

Set the frame before the first click — what this is, and the one sentence that explains why it's built the way it's built.

Say this

“This is All Paws Inn — a boutique pet boarding hotel in Clear Lake, Houston. We built it from a competitive research pass across fifty real pet-hospitality websites, so nothing here is a generic template — every decision traces back to something the research showed either works or fails in this category.”

“Two things make it different from almost everything we looked at. First, it treats dogs and cats as genuinely equal guests, not a dog site with a cat page bolted on. Second, it never makes you call for a price — every rate is on the page, including holidays, a year out.”

The walkthrough

Live demo path

Eight stops, in the order that makes the case best. Each one names the page, gives you something to physically do, a line to say, and — where it's not obvious — why that feature exists at all.

01
/ — homepage

The split hero

Load the homepage. Don't scroll yet — let the hero sit on screen for a second.

Point out the diptych: a dog on the left, a cat on the right, one hairline seam down the middle. Equal size, equal weight, one photo each.

“Most sites in this category default to a dog hero and mention cats somewhere below the fold. Here the split itself is the pitch — before you've read a word, you already know neither species is the afterthought.”

Then use the arrive/leave/guest bar right in the hero to plug in two dates and hit “See rooms and rates.” It drops straight into the planner with those dates already filled in — no separate “check availability” step.

02
/ — Two Houses section

Two Houses, one toggle

Scroll to the room index. Click the Dog/Cat toggle back and forth a couple of times.

“This is the same component doing double duty — same arch-shaped frame, same four-tier list, same layout. Toggle to cats and it's not a smaller, quieter version of the dog side. It's the Cattery: its own four rooms, its own pricing, its own photography.”

Why it matters: this is the page that most pet sites get wrong first — cats get one paragraph near the bottom. Here the primary room-browsing UI is built to hold both from the start.

03
/ — A Day at the Inn

A day, told twice

Scrub through the hourly timeline for dogs — yard turnout, group play, quiet time. Then flip the toggle to cats.

“Watch what actually changes here — it's not the same six sentences with 'dog' swapped for 'cat.' A cat's day is structured completely differently: no group play at all, ten minutes of hunting-style play instead of an hour outside, and a four-hour untouched stretch in the middle because that's what sixteen hours of normal sleep actually needs.”
04
/quiet-wing

The Quiet Wing

Navigate to the Quiet Wing page — the one section of the homepage that breaks into a dark panel, and its own dedicated page.

“This is the feature almost nobody else in the category builds. Six rooms, behind their own soundproofed door, for animals who can't handle a normal boarding day — reactive dogs, cats who've never boarded anywhere successfully, senior animals, anyone medically fragile. One assigned caregiver, no group play expected of them, and it's open to cats exactly as much as dogs.”

The number that lands: it's $18 a night over the base room, and the copy says plainly that's what the extra staffing costs — not a premium tier marked up for anxious owners.

05
/plan — Stay Planner

Plan a Stay, start to finish

Run the full flow live: pick a dog, pick dates that land on a real holiday week if you can (Thanksgiving or Christmas week both work), choose a room, add one or two enrichment options, then finish on the details step.

“Watch the total build in the sidebar as we go — nothing is hidden until a final screen. And because we picked a peak week, you'll see the holiday surcharge appear as its own line, at the exact rate published on the rates page. It's the same number everywhere it shows up, because it's computed from one source, not typed in five different places.”

If you land on a fully-booked room: that's real too — the planner will offer the nearest available dates instead of a dead end. Worth showing once if it comes up naturally.

06
/cattery

The Cattery, on its own

Visit the Cattery page directly, independent of the toggle. Let the different lighting and pacing register.

“This isn't the dog page in a different color. It's cooler light, more air in the layout, a slower rhythm — and the headline claim is literal: no dog has ever been inside this building. Separate entrance, separate air handling, and the page says so in the first line.”
07
/portal · /den-cam

Returning guest: Portal and Den Cam

Sign into the Guest Portal with anything — it's a demo login, any email and password work. Show the upcoming stay, then click into one of the two pet profiles and point out the vaccine-expiry warning on one of them.

“This is what a customer sees on their third or fourth stay, not their first. Two pets on the account, a vaccine that's about to lapse flagged automatically, and a full history of past report cards.”

Then open the Den Cam page and show the signed-out, locked state before signing in.

“We were deliberate about this — it's labeled as a sample frame, not a live feed, because it isn't one. We'd rather be honest about what's simulated than let it look like a real camera stream.”
08
/rates

Rates, in full

Close on the rates page. Scroll to the peak-dates table.

“This is the whole price list — every room, every add-on, the grooming menu, the shuttle fares, and every holiday surcharge window with its exact dates, published a year ahead. Nothing behind a form, nothing that says 'call for pricing.' That single habit is the most common complaint the research turned up about this entire category, and it's the easiest one to just not do.”

If you have more time, or someone asks

Feature reference

Not part of the core path — pull any of these up if the conversation goes there.

/kitchen

The Kitchen

  • Their own food, prepared and served, at no charge — stated first, on purpose
  • A real cooked-to-order menu after that, dog and cat sections both
  • Everything shown is dog-safe: no onion, garlic, grapes, or chocolate anywhere
/spa

The Spa

  • Priced by coat condition, not by breed
  • Separate dog and cat sections and separate teams
  • “If they say no, we stop” — no sedation, ever, stated as policy
/shuttle

The Airport Run

  • Hobby is the close airport from Clear Lake (~20 min); IAH is the long one (~65 min) — the copy reflects that, not a generic “we're near the airport” line
  • Neighborhood runs to League City, Friendswood, Pearland, Nassau Bay & Seabrook
/safety

Care & Safety

  • Staffing ratios, certifications, sanitation schedule, spelled out rather than badge-stamped
  • A real hurricane protocol — genuinely relevant for Clear Lake, not boilerplate
  • A separate summer heat protocol for Houston conditions
/new-guests

Before Your First Stay

  • Vaccine requirements stated plainly, dogs and cats both
  • An interactive packing checklist that remembers what you've ticked
  • Meet & greet booking, presented as free and low-pressure
/policies · /credits

Policies & Credits

  • Cancellation, medication, and behavior policy in plain language
  • Full photography credit list and an explicit demo-site disclosure

Anticipated questions

Is All Paws Inn a real business?

No — fully fictional, built as a design and engineering demonstration. That's disclosed on the credits page and in the footer of every page.

Is there a real backend? Can someone actually book?

No backend. The planner, portal, and Den Cam are fully built frontend experiences using deterministic mock data, so numbers stay consistent everywhere they appear — but nothing submits anywhere. The point was to prove the flow end-to-end, not stand up a booking system.

Why Houston specifically, and why Clear Lake?

The address is real — 1051 Pineloch Dr, Clear Lake, Houston — and the site was rebuilt around it: real airport distances, a real hurricane-season safety story, real neighborhood names for the shuttle routes. It's not a generic city name dropped into a template.

How was the content decided — is any of it guesswork?

It started from a 50-site competitive research report, ranked and analyzed for what the strongest pet-boarding sites do well and where the weaker ones fail. Every major feature traces back to a specific finding in that research.

What about accessibility?

Zero violations on an automated accessibility audit across all 18 routes, plus manual keyboard-navigation testing on the planner, the mega-menu, and the calendar.

That's deliberate: it's built the way a real production site would be structured, even though nothing behind it is real yet.”
